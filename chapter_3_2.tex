\section*{Under the Gilded Heavens}

Their conversation was suddenly interrupted by the heavy thud of boots echoing down the timber-ribbed corridor outside. A burly ship's mate strode past the cabin doors, swinging a brass handbell that clattered brightly over the ambient noise of the lower decks.

\enquote{Dinner is ready! Dinner is ready!} the man's gruff, sea-salted voice called out. \enquote{Gather in the common area under the sterncastle! Hot supper awaits!}

Almost immediately upon the sailor's passing, a frantic, joyful clatter of footsteps heralded a new arrival. A young girl came tearing down the corridor, her movement a chaotic, rambunctious mix of a brisk walk and an outright run. She possessed a boundless, kinetic energy, her eyes darting eagerly as she searched the doors. She skidded to a halt in front of the girls' cabin, peering into the empty room with a loud, theatrical sigh of disappointment.

From the doorway of the boys' cabin, Celine caught sight of her and leaned out. \enquote{Philippa! Over here!}

Philippa spun on her heel, her face lighting up with a brilliant grin as she bounded over the raised wooden threshold into Thomas and Baldwin's cabin.

\enquote{There you are!} Philippa declared, out of breath but thoroughly delighted.

\enquote{Philippa, come in,} Cecile said smoothly, taking on the role of gracious mediator. She gestured toward the two boys. \enquote{Allow me to introduce you. This is Baldwin Attewell, and this is Thomas Blackwood. Boys, this is Philippa.}

Baldwin offered a polite, somewhat stiff nod, his spectacles slipping slightly down his nose, while Thomas offered a brief, relaxed smile, his piercing grey eyes taking in the girl's energetic demeanour.

\enquote{A pleasure,} Thomas murmured, his voice calm and grounded.

\enquote{Brilliant to meet you both, truly,} Philippa said, waving off the formalities with a frantic motion of her hands. \enquote{But did you hear the man? Dinner is ready! We must go right this instant before the older students eat the best of it!}

Cecile nodded in firm agreement, smoothing the front of her robes. \enquote{She is absolutely right. I suggest we hurry. This will be the last time we enjoy a truly good meal until we drop anchor in Dublin.}

Baldwin blinked, adjusting his glasses. \enquote{Why is that?}

\enquote{Because tonight's feast was prepared on shore, back in London, right before we left the pier,} Cecile explained as she ushered them out into the corridor. \enquote{Over the next few days, however, our meals will be prepared in the ship's galley. I assure you, the conditions down there are much fewer, the heat is stifling, and the food will have noticeably less taste. So, eat well tonight.}

Motivated by Cecile's pragmatic warning, the quintet made their way through the belly of the ship. They navigated the tight, shadowy corridors, the wooden floorboards swaying gently beneath their feet as the vessel cut through the currents of the Thames. At the end of the passage, they climbed a narrow, steep set of wooden stairs that protested with loud creaks under their weight, finally emerging into the small and cozy common room situated beneath the towering sterncastle.

It was long and slightly curved, filled with narrow wooden tables flanked by sturdy benches. The room was bathed in a warm, flickering, golden glow emanating from thick beeswax candles and polished brass olive oil lamps mounted on iron sconces along the bulkheads. The air was thick and heady with the scent of roasting meats, baked bread, the sharp tang of the burning olive oil, and the faint, ever-present aroma of sea salt and waxed timber. Small, thick-paned windows lined the port and starboard sides, offering glimpses of the dark world outside.

But it was the ceiling that commanded true awe.

Spanning the entirety of the common room's curved roof was a breathtaking, stylised map of the heavens. It was a masterpiece that bridged earlier Romanesque style with the soaring, decorative elegance of later Gothic styles. Rather than a realistic depiction of the night sky, it was a symbolic, iconographic tapestry. The background was painted in a deep, flat, and impossibly rich expanse of ultramarine and dark indigo.

Most of the stars and a slender, delicate crescent moon were rendered in gleaming silver. However, the great navigational anchors of the sky---Polaris, Ursa Minor, Ursa Major, Cassiopeia, and Orion---were distinguished by heavy, textured gold. They were painted in oversized, mythical shapes: a great gilded bear, a smaller bear, a crowned queen, a towering hunter.

The true magic of the ceiling, however, lay in its enchantment. It was vibrantly alive. Wisps of painted, silver-lined clouds moved slowly across the indigo expanse. As the ship rocked on the waves and shifted its course along the river, the golden constellations slid accordingly across the timber canvas. It was not a literal tool for the helmsman, but a shifting, comforting representation of the very stars the sailors relied upon above deck.

The five of them managed to secure a prime spot at a table pushed right up against one of the small windows. Sitting in the centre of the scarred wooden surface was a beautifully carved Pachisi board game. Thomas quietly pushed it aside to make room for the pewter trenchers and wooden cups that a passing steward was hastily laying out.

While they waited for the main platters to arrive, they peered out the thick glass of the window. The ship had well and truly left the bustling docks behind and was navigating the wider, darker stretches of the Thames. It was the dead of night. Looking back, the immense, hulking shadow of the Tower of London was slowly receding into the distance, its imposing Norman stone barely distinguishable from the black sky, save for the flickering orange pinpricks of torches burning on its battlements. The city of London was a fading cluster of embers.

Out here on the water, the river was pitch black, a rippling mirror of obsidian that caught only the pale reflection of the real moon and the occasional, scattered bonfires burning lonely vigils on the muddy banks of the estuary.

\section*{Hepburn}

As they turned their attention back to the warmth of the room, the crowd parted slightly, revealing a massive, commanding presence near the front of the hall. It was Professor Humphry Hepburn, the corpulent Hogwarts Headmaster. He was a large man with a remarkably warm disposition, currently laughing boisterously with a group of nervous-looking first and second-year students.

Thomas, ever observant, leaned across the table toward Cecile. \enquote{Is that Professor Hepburn?} he asked, his voice low over the din of the room. \enquote{What is he like? His classes, I mean, and him in general.}

Baldwin adjusted his spectacles, looking thoughtful. \enquote{I've seen him a few times in Diagon Alley this past summer. Once, he was buying up armfuls potion ingredients and supplies, and he looked right as rain. But another time, I saw him walking out of what seemed a council meeting in the upper rooms of the Leaky Cauldron\ldots{} he looked utterly miserable. He definitely seems happier when he's just buying items for his potions.}

Cecile smiled knowingly, resting her elbows on the wooden table. \enquote{It is no surprise to see him down here tonight, eating with us,} she said. \enquote{He could have easily dined hours ago with the other professors in the captain's quarters. But he doesn't operate like that. He takes particular care in dealing with the younger students.}

She leaned in slightly, lowering her voice so as not to be overheard by the older students nearby. \enquote{He's a Hufflepuff, you know, and brilliant at Potions and Alchemy. But he absolutely despises politics and bureaucracy, which is probably why he looked so miserable at that Council meeting. He is incredibly warm and good-natured. Some say he is a bit too relaxed with the rules, especially with the first-years.}

\enquote{Relaxed?} Philippa perked up, her eyes gleaming with the promise of future mischief. \enquote{I like the sound of that.}

\enquote{Don't get too comfortable,} Cecile warned, though she was smiling. \enquote{Hepburn might be forgiving, but he relies on his staff to keep order. Professor Agnes Ravenclaw and Professor Hugh Ligier are entirely different beasts. They are much, much stricter. Ligier is intense, and Professor Ravenclaw\ldots{}} Cecile paused, her eyes glinting with a bit of shared gossip. \enquote{Well, I have the distinct impression that Agnes Ravenclaw believes \textit{she} ought to be the Headmistress instead, and she runs her classes with an iron fist.}

Thomas, who had been listening quietly while watching the magical gold-leaf stars shift above them, spoke up, his tone mild but laced with genuine curiosity. \enquote{Who among them is the most senior? Professor Ravenclaw or Headmaster Hepburn?}

Cecile looked at Thomas, a playful smirk breaking across her face. \enquote{Honestly? Looking at how they carry themselves\ldots{} neither of them looks a day older than a hundred and twenty.}

There was a beat of silence as the mental image registered, and then Baldwin snorted loudly. Philippa burst into a bright, ringing laugh, Celine and Cecile quickly joined in, and even Thomas let out a soft, genuine chuckle, his shoulders relaxing.

As their laughter subsided, Cecile rested her elbows on the table, eager to share what she knew from her time at the school. \enquote{But in all honesty, Professor Ravenclaw is a formidable presence. She teaches History of Magic and is the Head of Ravenclaw House. She's actually an indirect descendant of Rowena Ravenclaw and a pure-blood, but she entirely disregards blood politics. She openly acknowledges that some of the most consequential wizards in history were not pure-bloods, and she's fiercely proud of having earned her position through rigorous scholarship.}

Baldwin's eyes lit up behind his spectacles. \enquote{What are her classes like?}

\enquote{Severe and uncompromising,} Cecile warned, looking pointedly at the energetic Philippa. \enquote{She is a strict disciplinarian who believes that learning requires absolute order, silence, and focus. She won't coddle you; she treats first-years like adult scholars and expects you to behave as such. When it comes to history, she insists on objective facts and has a deep disdain for mythology or filling in the blanks with compelling narratives---it is verifiable truth or nothing.}

\enquote{Sounds intense,} Thomas murmured, though he looked intrigued.

\enquote{She is brilliant, though,} Cecile continued. \enquote{Her classroom is packed with original manuscripts, maps, and documents. And while she possesses a massive private library just like Headmaster Hepburn, she operates differently: every single one of her books and documents has a copy available in the Hogwarts library for the students to use.} Cecile paused, a knowing glint in her eye. \enquote{Though, I wouldn't go digging into the dark wizarding history sections just yet. She places alert spells on the dangerous books to monitor exactly who is trying to access them.}

\enquote{Brilliant and cautious,} Baldwin nodded in earnest appreciation.

\enquote{Exactly. She is the school's primary disciplinarian and the necessary counterweight to Headmaster Hepburn. She respects his administrative capabilities but finds his leniency towards the students highly irritating. While Hepburn is universally loved, Agnes Ravenclaw is respected and feared. Oh, and one more thing\ldots{}} Cecile lowered her voice to a conspiratorial whisper. \enquote{She is a registered Animagus, her animal form is a white raven.}

Before Philippa could gasp at that revelation, the loud clatter of pewter trenchers interrupted them as the first massive platters of roasted capon and spiced root vegetables were finally set down upon their table, pulling their attention to the feast before them.

The heavy, spiced aromas of the English feast filled the space beneath the shifting, gilded ceiling. Cecile wasted no time, piling her pewter trencher high with thick slices of roasted capon, dark gravy, and buttery root vegetables, eating with unabashed enthusiasm. Celine, however, eyed the heavy local fare with far less zeal. She delicately picked at a crust of bread and a small portion of vegetables, her expression suggesting she was already deeply missing the culinary of her continental home.

As they ate, the lively hum of the common room wrapped around them, insulating their table from the chill of the river outside. Baldwin swallowed a bite of chicken and thoughtfully adjusted his spectacles.

\enquote{Speaking of the professors,} Baldwin began, leaning slightly over his plate, \enquote{I saw another one in Diagon Alley a few weeks ago. Professor Saoirse Millan. Someone at the apothecary mentioned she was an Animagus as well. Is that right?}

Cecile swallowed her food and wiped her mouth with a napkin, her eyes immediately lighting up with admiration. \enquote{Oh, absolutely. Her animal form is a magnificent red kite. And honestly, it is almost as beautiful and radiant as she is in her human form.}

Thomas looked up from his meal, his curiosity piqued again. \enquote{What does she teach?}

\enquote{Care of Magical Creatures,} Cecile replied warmly. \enquote{She is stunningly beautiful, but she couldn't care less about the traditional expectations of how a lady ought to dress or behave. She is quite \textit{petite}, small---hardly taller than some of us, really---and she has this striking, short red hair. You will rarely see her in formal robes. She is an absolute tomboy and wears highly practical clothing, usually trousers with folded hems.}

\enquote{Trousers?} Celine asked, her eyebrows rising slightly in surprise at the breach of 14th-century etiquette.

\enquote{Yes, and she even prefers to go entirely barefoot whenever she is out on the grass, though she will grudgingly put on shoes when she has to walk inside the castle.} Cecile laughed softly. \enquote{She frequently climbs the trees around the grounds just so she can sit closer to the animals in the canopy. Because of all this, she doesn't live in the castle with the other staff. She lives in a rustic hut just outside the walls, right on the edge of the Forbidden Forest.}

Philippa, who was eagerly devouring a roasted root vegetable, leaned forward, completely enchanted. \enquote{She sounds brilliant.}

\enquote{She is,} Cecile agreed. \enquote{She has this incredible energy about her. She doesn't yell at the beasts; she just makes these sharp, corrective `tsst' sounds, and even creatures massively larger than she is will fall right into line, as if she were their pack-leader. She keeps a very diverse collection of creatures around her hut: a very large white owl, a highly aloof cat, and a magical raven-like bird that can perfectly mimic any vocal or animal sound it hears.}

\enquote{That sounds like a noisy hut,} Baldwin noted with a grin.

\enquote{Oh, you have no idea,} Cecile said, her grin matching his. \enquote{The best part is her dragon. It is still young, and it behaves exactly like an overgrown puppy. It actually plays fetch, rolls over for belly rubs, and firmly believes it is part of her pack. At night, it sleeps with its heavy head resting right inside the doorway of her hut. It snores incredibly loudly, but Professor Millan says she finds the sound relaxing.}

Thomas chuckled, imagining the petite witch sleeping soundly next to a snoring, fire-breathing dragon. \enquote{I suppose a professor like that doesn't spend much time reading in the library?}

\enquote{She actually owns very few books of her own,} Cecile pointed out, her eyes dancing with amusement. \enquote{Mostly because her puppy dragon ate half of them.}

The table erupted into another round of laughter.

\enquote{She is wonderful,} Cecile concluded, her tone softening with genuine respect. \enquote{Some of the Slytherin students like to mock her for her unconventional ways, but the truth is, almost everyone else absolutely loves her. She has a wonderful spirit. In fact, sometimes she doesn't even bother using the front doors to enter the castle; she just flies straight through an open window as a red kite and transforms back into her human form right there in the middle of the corridor.}

The laughter around the table slowly subsided, blending back into the warm, ambient noise of the common room. The mention of Professor Millan soaring through the castle windows as a red kite, however, had left a distinct sparkle in Philippa's eyes. She practically bounced on her wooden bench, momentarily ignoring the roasted vegetables on her trencher.

\enquote{Speaking of flying,} Philippa interrupted, her voice brimming with restless enthusiasm. \enquote{I am absolutely counting the minutes until I can get on a broom. What are the flying lessons like at Hogwarts? Who teaches us?}

Cecile set down her wooden cup, a smile playing on her lips. \enquote{You are in for a treat, Philippa. Our flying instructor, and race team coach, is a man named Afonso da Senna. He is Portuguese, though I think he comes from an Italian family. And he isn't just a teacher---he is a three-time course race champion. People often call him `the fastest wizard alive,' or the fastest of all time.}

Baldwin's eyes widened behind his spectacles. \enquote{The fastest wizard alive? Does he win every race he enters?}

\enquote{Not necessarily,} Cecile clarified, cutting a piece of capon. \enquote{There are many brilliant course race flyers out there, but he is a legend among them.}

\enquote{What does the fastest wizard alive look like?} Thomas asked, genuinely curious. Given the imposing figures of some of the other professors, he was picturing a towering, broad-shouldered man.

\enquote{Actually, he is quite short---perhaps a hundred and sixty-five centimetres---and very slim. He purposely keeps himself in absolute peak physical shape to minimise his weight and maximise his speed. Flying at those high speeds requires incredible physical conditioning.} Cecile pointed a fork toward the window, out at the dark night. \enquote{He spends so many hours training outside that his skin is quite tanned. He has dark brown hair and light brown eyes, and he is generally a very quiet, shy person. Not awkward, just quiet. But when he does speak about flying, he is highly eloquent.}

\enquote{Is he a strict teacher?} Celine asked, looking slightly apprehensive at the thought of a world-champion instructor.

\enquote{He is an absolute perfectionist,} Cecile said, leaning in. \enquote{His dedication is almost an obsession. He pays attention to the absolute minimum details. He will analyse the exact angle of your grip, your posture, and even make micro-adjustments to the twigs on your broom. He leaves nothing to chance. He only likes course racing, by the way. He claims straight-line racing is boring and that only course racing truly rewards the skill of the wizard.}

Philippa looked thrilled. \enquote{A real racing course?}

\enquote{Yes, but don't expect to memorise it,} Cecile warned with a laugh. \enquote{He ensures the Hogwarts race track is never permanent. He changes it continuously throughout the year just to prevent the students from getting complacent.}

Baldwin frowned thoughtfully. \enquote{If he is such a legendary racer, why is he teaching first-years at a school?}

\enquote{Well, the official story is that professional racing isn't very lucrative yet, so he needs the steady income. Plus, the castle grounds provide unparalleled space and conditions for his own private training.} Cecile lowered her voice conspiratorially. \enquote{But the secret truth is that he genuinely loves teaching basic flying lessons, especially to young students just starting out. He actually takes a quiet pride in breaking down and correcting the ingrained bad habits of students who think they already know how to fly before coming to school.}

She looked pointedly at Philippa, who simply grinned, entirely undeterred.

\enquote{He also has a brilliant dynamic with Professor Millan,} Cecile added, her tone softening. \enquote{Because they both love the outdoors, they meet frequently outside the castle walls and fly together. Afonso will ride alongside her on one of his heavily modified brooms, while she flies as a red kite or rides her puppy dragon. Honestly, I think he secretly envies her.}

\enquote{Envy her?} Thomas asked. \enquote{Why? If he's a master mechanic with the best brooms in the world?}

\enquote{Because she can fly as a bird,} Cecile explained simply. \enquote{He admires her natural ability to experience flight without relying on a tool. But until he figures out how to grow wings, he will just have to settle for being the fastest man on a broom.}

The image of the world's fastest wizard trying to grow wings kept the table in high spirits for a few moments longer, until the loud scrape of a heavy wooden chair shifting against the floorboards drew their attention.

\section*{Marzipan Phoenix}

At the front of the common room, Headmaster Hepburn had risen to his feet. He clapped his large, meaty hands together, the resounding noise easily cutting through the lively chatter of the hall.

\enquote{Right then!} Hepburn announced, his booming voice echoing off the painted, starry ceiling. His tone was incredibly warm, but it carried the firm, unquestionable authority of a Headmaster. \enquote{The sea breeze is picking up, and tomorrow brings a long day of travel. It is time for all first and second-year students to return to your cabins for the night. Off you go, now. Sleep well!}

A collective, albeit reluctant, groan rose from some of the younger tables, but the students obediently began to rise. Thomas, Baldwin, Celine, Cecile, and Philippa gathered their cloaks and joined the flow of students heading toward the main stairwell. The gentle rocking of the ship had indeed grown slightly more pronounced as they left the sheltered estuary for the open water.

At the landing, the group had to part ways. Philippa offered a bright, energetic wave before bounding up the carved wooden steps toward her private cabin in the sterncastle.

Thomas, Baldwin, and the two French girls turned the opposite way, carefully descending the narrow, creaking stairs back down into the shadowy, timber-ribbed corridors of the middle deck. The air down here was cooler, smelling sharply of brine and the protective tar coating the hull.

They reached the girls' door first. Celine, looking thoroughly exhausted from the day's travels, leaned against the doorframe.

\enquote{Good night, Thomas. Good night, Baldwin,} Cecile said gracefully, offering them a warm smile. \enquote{It was lovely talking with you both.}

\enquote{Good night,} Thomas replied smoothly, while Baldwin offered a polite nod and a tired, \enquote{See you in the morning.}

Leaving the girls to their rest, Thomas and Baldwin walked the short distance to their own door and pushed the curtain open. Both boys stopped in their tracks, pleasantly surprised. While they had been eating, the ship's stewards---or perhaps a bit of helpful shipboard magic---had been at work. The narrow, utilitarian berth beds had been neatly made up, the heavy woollen blankets turned down invitingly against the chill of the sea air.

But it was the small detail resting on the wooden ledge beside their pillows that caught Thomas's eye. Sitting on a tiny square of parchment was a beautifully crafted marzipan cake, shaped perfectly like a sleeping phoenix.

Thomas picked his up, admiring the delicate detailing of the marzipan feathers. He took a bite, and the sharp, bright flavours of ginger and cool mint burst across his tongue, leaving a pleasant, warming sensation in his chest that perfectly countered the damp cold of the cabin. It was absolutely delicious.

Finishing the treat in two bites, Thomas wiped his fingers, feeling a deep sense of contentment settle over him. He moved around the small cabin, leaning over to blow out the thick beeswax candles and twisting the brass knobs on the olive oil lamps until the flames sputtered and died, plunging the room into comfortable darkness.

He climbed into his berth, finally allowing the tight, constant mental grip on his Metamorphmagus features to relax in the shadows.

\enquote{Good night, Baldwin,} Thomas said quietly into the dark, the rhythmic creak of the ship's hull already lulling him to sleep.

\enquote{Night, Thomas,} Baldwin murmured from across the cabin, his voice heavy with sleep.
